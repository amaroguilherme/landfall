## High-Level Project Summary

LANDFALL is offline-first health-monitoring software for exploration crews who cannot wait for Houston. NASA-STD-3001 leaves alert numbers to each program, so LANDFALL checks every crewmember against NASA's limits and NOAA's storm scale, each cited to its source, and against their own baseline at a measured chance rate, labelling every number it chose as ours. Three guided cases (radiation shelter, renal stone risk, sleep) turn a finding into one countermeasure, a tripwire, onboard escalation and a consent-filtered ground packet that waits for the link; each prints as a paper cue card. The demo puts a simulated Mars crew in a modelled solar conjunction, replays the real October 1989 proton event and re-runs the RAD team's storm rule on Curiosity's 14-year record.

## Detailed Project Description

*Figure 1. Ask Houston, or act onboard.*

### The problem: 20 light-minutes from help

MET 412/14:20:00 on the Mars surface, 18 March 2028. Mars is inside a solar-conjunction window modelled from JPL Horizons: a light-speed round trip takes 39 min 41 s, and Houston's earliest reply is 11 d 06 h away. At 14:15 an external space-weather monitor in Mars orbit reached NOAA's S3 level (1,000 pfu, ≥10 MeV), replaying the real GOES-7 record of 19 October 1989. NASA-STD-3001 has ground medical support clear crewmembers "prior to each EVA" ([V1 6006]); in conjunction, no one on the ground can.

NASA's rationale for radiation alerts: "The crew must be alerted even if ground communication is disrupted or is unviable" ([V2 6164]). Yet NASA-STD-3001 sets no number for an in-flight crew-health alert: it says "outside safe limits" ([V2 6022], [V2 11036]) or "exceed predefined thresholds" ([V2 6164]), and leaves in-mission sensorimotor fitness-for-duty limits to operations ([V1 4006], [V1 4008]). So LANDFALL labels every number with who published it.

### How it works

**Evaluate.** NASA publishes three kinds of limit, which LANDFALL never merges: exposure ceilings (Table 4.8-1 doses in mGy-Eq or mGy, never mSv), outcome limits and fitness-for-duty floors. Simulated CM-2's VO₂max of 35.1 ml/min/kg meets the 32.9 floor for microgravity EVA ([V1 4001]) and misses the 36.5 for surface EVA ([V1 4002]), so at Mars it reads "EVA fitness floor not met" (clearance is ground medical support's, [V1 6006]). *Tier A* holds 35 rules (31 alerting): 28 from NASA-STD-3001 with their source text verbatim, NOAA's S1–S5 (scale thresholds, not limits), the HRP renal evidence report's urine target and one design choice, NOAA's S3 number at Mars. Severity follows Vol 2 Table 10.3-1: LANDFALL's only Warning ("requires immediate action") is S3 or above; a limit not met is a Caution ("needs attention, but not immediate action"); it never raises an Emergency. *Tier B* tests eleven alerting indicators (PVT-B, sleep, resting heart rate, HRV, urine and fluid volume, cuff blood pressure, fingerstick glucose) against the crewmember's own 14-day baseline with Crawford & Howell's (1998) single-case t-test: a day outside the 99.9 % prediction interval (|t| > 4.221) is a Caution at most.

*Figure 2. One reading, two floors.*

**We audited our own statistics, twice.** The textbook Jacobson–Truax index on a crewmember's own baseline flagged an unchanged indicator on about 6.6 % of days (14-day baseline, r = 0.8), not the 0.1 % printed. With realistic noise (published within-person variability where it exists), raw PVT-B lapse counts and an absolute sleep-midpoint shift raised chance cautions about 10 and 12.7 times as often as printed. Now counts are tested on the Anscombe scale, the midpoint as a signed shift both ways, and each indicator's chance rate is measured and printed beside its band: in all, about one chance caution per crewmember every 161 days. A case opened by a single flag reads "Possibly chance: about 1 in 1000 days…", and a re-measure checks it. Autocorrelation raises real rates (about 3.7× at a day-to-day correlation of 0.5), so every printed rate is a lower bound. On the simulator's noise, Tier B flags 1 of the 6 scripted alerting signals; CM-1's sleep case opened on a scripted schedule shift, not on the shortened sleep.

*Figure 3. Every number, its basis.*

**Act.** A trigger reaching Caution or Warning opens its case: interpretation, one primary countermeasure, guided steps (required ones must be done, or declined with a reason, before an outcome counts), a reassessment tripwire and "Why this fired" (Appendix F F.6.2 g). A red-flag "yes" or a tripped tripwire escalates: tier 1, the onboard Crew Medical Officer, now; tier 2, the flight surgeon, through a consent-filtered HL7 FHIR R4 packet sent "if communication is available or upon being restored" ([V2 6164] rationale). If the Crew Medical Officer is the patient, tier 1 is the Commander, the crew's second Crew Medical Officer ([V1 3006] requires at least two; who is second is our choice). Escalations and overdue reassessments (our timing rule) also raise annunciator alerts, NEW until acknowledged.

*Figure 4. Why this fired.*

Cases: a **solar particle event** (NOAA S3 near Earth, the same 1,000 pfu on the Mars-orbit monitor, or blood-forming-organ dose near its 30-day limit: stop EVA and shelter; it is the health case that follows the vehicle's radiation alarm, [V2 6164]); **renal stone risk** (urine below 1.5 L/day, where the HRP evidence report says risk rises: first a required red-flag question, escalating on "yes", then drink more); **sleep and circadian drift** (PVT-B and sleep: light per [V2 8055] Table 8.7-2).

**LANDFALL BAND.** We found no operational wearable sensor specification: the ExMC Mars medical ConOps (NTRS 20200001715) assumes "All crewmembers are wearing biosensors" and specifies none. Our answer is a block diagram and BOM, never built, referenced to the flown CSA Bio-Monitor. Its core is the NOT MEASURABLE HERE boundary, which hands urine, dose, chemistry, blood pressure and bone density to other sources.

**The RAD team's storm warning, replayed.** #/radiation replays the nowcasting rule the MSL RAD team published (Löwe et al. 2025): alert when detector E passes 1.25 times its 5-day background, confirm on the next sample. On Curiosity's whole RAD record at NASA's iSWA (390,270 samples, 2012-08-17 to 2026-11-12), of the paper's 16 Table 1 surface rows 7 reproduce, 1 deviates (2014-09-02, −33.2 %), 4 of its triggers are missed and 4 non-triggers agree; every difference is printed. On 20 May 2024, the record's largest storm, the rule confirms 17 minutes after onset, before any reply from Earth could arrive (at least 31.7 minutes), and its total SEP dose is 0.8 % above the paper's. Over the record the rule triggers 13 times, at 1.3× to 59.7× background. The replayed storm, part of [V1 4031]'s design-reference event, had witnesses: STS-34's five astronauts, on Atlantis from 18 to 23 October 1989.

**Paper cue cards.** [V2 10130] requires "alternative information sources for use in the event of the loss of the information management system"; its rationale names "paper cue cards". LANDFALL prints one per case (#/cards). Its triggers, steps, tripwire, escalation tiers and refusals come only from the rules files the engine evaluates; it gives no medication or dosing advice, prints no mood data and carries the rules' digest: a different digest on screen means reprint.

**Coverage.** Weighted by likelihood, 13.6 % of NASA's sample Integrated Medical Model output (Vol 1 Table E.2-1, 48 conditions) is watched by a case, 23.2 % is asked about daily and 6.3 % more by a symptom only; 56.9 % is NOT MEASURABLE HERE, each row naming where it is known from. Watched or asked does not mean detected.

### What we hope to achieve

The Crew Medical Officer ("CMOs often are, but are not required, to be physicians", [V1 5003] rationale) gets one plain-English action and its reason; flight surgeons, a coded packet; programs, the numbers they must set. Human Research Roadmap gap EIHSO-301 (open, Risk 175) asks NASA to "establish the thresholds" for "on-board, intelligent systems that will support crew health and performance". LANDFALL proposes that layer; its thresholds are data a program can replace. Next: analog-crew data, a bench-built BAND, usability tests with non-physicians.

The idea comes from navigation: NASA tested a sextant on the ISS because "crews need the capability to navigate autonomously in the event of lost communication with the ground" (PI Greg Holt). LANDFALL is that backup for health.

### Tools and verification

A stdlib-only Python 3.12 reference engine and a TypeScript browser engine agree on all 6,460 shared conformance vectors to 1e-9 (app: React 19, Vite, uPlot). 1,373 Python tests (pytest, uv; 1,365 on a fresh clone, without the source cache) and 7,011 TypeScript tests (Vitest) pass, checking each NASA and NOAA number against frozen source text. Static files, offline after one visit. Also NASA Horizon Design System tokens, Cloudflare, Claude Code.

## Use of NASA Data

Remove the NASA data and LANDFALL loses its Tier A rules, its light time and conjunction, its radiation record, its hazard and risk text and its real lab ranges. Build-time adapters freeze each source as JSON with retrieval time, SHA-256 and caveats; all 26 snapshots are listed at #/data. NASA does not endorse this project, and no NASA insignia is used.

- **NASA-STD-3001 Vol 1 Rev C** (NASA OCHMO, approved 2023-09-15). The verbatim text of 20 requirements, the FFD/PEL/POL glossary, Table 4.8-1 and Appendix E's Table E.2-1, extracted with a ToUnicode-aware PDF parser from a PDF identified by SHA-256. It drives the FFD ([V1 4001], [V1 4002]), POL ([V1 4027]) and PEL rules ([V1 4030]; Table 4.8-1 organ limits in mGy-Eq or mGy, never mSv), the vehicle-design context rules ([V1 4031]–[V1 4033], shown but never fired), the delegations ([V1 4006], [V1 4008]), the care duties the cases and the BAND cite ([V1 3003], [V1 3004]), the crew's second Crew Medical Officer ([V1 3006]: "a minimum of two crewmembers per vehicle/platform", "to ensure that a backup is available for medical treatment") and ground medical clearance before each EVA ([V1 6006], cited on both VO₂max rules and quoted in "The problem"). The [V1 5003] rationale quoted in "What we hope to achieve" was read on p. 46 of the same PDF; it is not in the frozen snapshot. Table E.2-1, NASA's sample Integrated Medical Model output (48 conditions with a likelihood per mission), weights the coverage score, read with NASA's own limitation (E.2.3): "One limitation is that IMM was developed for application to ISS missions, but it may be applied to other design reference missions if the appropriate limitations are considered during interpretation of results." *In the app:* the category and citation on every finding, #/body, #/provenance, #/method (coverage: #/method?s=coverage).
- **NASA-STD-3001 Vol 2 Rev F** (NASA OCHMO, approved 2026-07-14; it replaced Rev E, 2025-05-01, at the same URL). 37 requirements and 7 tables, frozen verbatim. Used for:
  - cabin ppCO₂ ([V2 6004], one-hour average ≤ 3 mmHg) and suit PiCO₂ ([V2 11040], ≤ 20 mmHg in partial-gravity contingencies up to 1 h), filed as PEL by their shape (Vol 2 does not call them PELs);
  - radiation alerting and external space-weather monitoring ([V2 6164]; [V2 6165], whose rationale names "the August 1972 and October 1989 events"; the scenario's Mars-orbit monitor is the kind of external monitor that rationale describes, with one ≥10 MeV integral channel, not the proton and electron flux spectra [V2 6165] asks for);
  - the alert classes of Table 10.3-1, behind every severity, and [V2 10114]'s "distinct visual and audio annunciations": LANDFALL's alerts are visual only, a gap #/method declares;
  - Appendix F: colours (Tables F.5-1 and F.5-2), the MET format (F.6.1 f, "leading zeros are suppressed in day field": 412/14:20:00) and the explanation clause (F.6.2 g);
  - the lighting countermeasure ([V2 8055], Table 8.7-2);
  - water provision ([V2 6109], Table 6.3-1: a minimum of 2.5 L of potable water for hydration per crewmember per day, a supply requirement, not a urine target);
  - medical equipment usable by non-physicians ([V2 7045], quoted beside the crew's second Crew Medical Officer), stale data ([V2 10020]), privacy ([V2 10125]) and paper cue cards ([V2 10130]);
  - the BAND's leakage-current (Table 9.3-11) and touch-temperature (Tables 9.3-4, 9.3-5) limits;
  - searched negatives: no wearable, biosensor, EMI/EMC, battery-endurance or log-duration requirement, so the BAND's 72 h autonomy and 14-day log targets are design choices.

  *In the app:* colour on every screen, the rules, the cases, the cue cards, #/band, #/provenance, #/method.
- **NASA Human Research Program, official challenge resources:** *5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight*, *Risks of Human Spaceflight* and *Ongoing HRP Research in Space*, frozen and quoted verbatim. They supply the hazard and risk text in the body-region panels and each case's official risk ("Risk of Radiation-Induced Cancers", "Risk of Spaceflight-Induced Bone Changes", "Risk of Inadequate Sleep and Irregular Schedules"). The risks page lists 17 risks, none of them renal, which is why the renal case sits under bone. The research page answers the challenge's "Which measurements can be performed in space?": the Standard Measures wrist sensor for activity and light, ARCHeR actigraphy on Artemis II, and Tempus Pro (a past telemedicine investigation) as the BAND's blood-pressure hand-off example. *In the app:* #/body, #/band, #/data and the cases.
- **NASA Human Research Roadmap** (the Explore tree, 22 risk nodes, plus nine per-risk pages). Risk 81 "Risk of Renal Stone Formation" ("Active [current research ongoing]", last published 2025-05-13), Risk 100 (sleep) and Risk 175 "Risk of Adverse Mission Outcomes Due to Earth Independent Human-Systems Operations", with its open gap EIHSO-301. *In the app:* #/data and the cases.
- **HRP Evidence Report: Risk of Renal Stone Formation** (2017, NTRS 20170004709; 2024 edition, NTRS 20240012429). The source of the renal TARGET rule, cited as a NASA report with no requirement ID: risk "increases when urinary volume drops below 1.5 liters/day", and "an effective hydration countermeasure should be sufficient to produce 2.5-3 liters/day of urine volume" (citing a 2016 model by Kassemi and Thompson). The 2024 edition is a scan read by OCR; the 2017 edition carries the same sentence as text. *In the app:* #/provenance, the renal case and the Noon Sight fluids step.
- **NASA Open Science Data Repository, SpaceX Inspiration4.** OSD-575 (comprehensive metabolic panel, 19 analytes including glucose; 80 serum cytokines), OSD-569 (complete blood count, 20 analytes) and OSD-656 (203 urine inflammation proteins by NULISA-seq: not a urinalysis, so it never feeds the renal case). Values sit beside the reference range the laboratory printed for that same sample, because ranges change within a subject; cytokines have no clinical range and are shown without one. n = 4, about 3 days in LEO, no in-flight samples in these files: real reference ranges and a real recovery shape, not a long-duration model. *In the app:* #/body (OSD-575, OSD-569); #/data (all three).
- **JPL Horizons** (NASA JPL Solar System Dynamics Group). Daily Earth–Mars and Earth–Moon light time for 2026–2029 (Mars one-way 5.38 to 20.06 min). Conjunction windows are LANDFALL's model: the days when the Sun–Earth–Mars elongation is below 2.04°, a threshold fitted to six NASA/JPL published command moratoria from 2013 to 2023 (RMS edge error 0.79 days, worst 2.42). The scenario's 2028-03-12 to 2028-03-29 window is modelled, not a published moratorium. *In the app:* mission bar, scenario clock, hero and #/downlink.
- **NASA GSFC CCMC iSWA (HAPI): MSL RAD and LRO CRaTER.** Absorbed dose rates on the Mars surface and in lunar orbit, never converted to effective dose. The latest 90 days of MSL RAD detector E (median 184.7 µGy/day, 2026-08-14 to 2026-11-12; it may include the rover's RTG background) are the scenario's quiet-time baseline, and the habitat dose rate during the event scales it so that NOAA's S3 flux gives 10 × baseline: a labelled assumption, not a transport calculation. Curiosity's whole RAD record on iSWA (390,270 samples, 2012-08-17 to 2026-11-12; only 24 before 2013-03-22) carries the replay of the RAD team's published nowcasting rule (Löwe et al. 2025), compared row by row with the paper's Table 1; the adapter re-checks the paper's rule sentences and Table 1 rows word for word at every build. For 20 May 2024 the replay's total SEP dose, which the paper defines as the "background-removed accumulated SEP dose" of detector E, is 1,745.16 µGy against Table 1's 1,730.55 µGy. NASA's 10 June 2024 article says an astronaut beside Curiosity "would have received a radiation dose of 8,100 micrograys"; LANDFALL reproduces the paper's figure, not that one, and does not reconcile the two. *In the app:* the SPE scenario and #/radiation.
- **NASA SRAG (JSC) ISS TEPC**, via GSFC SPDF CDAWeb (HAPI): measured ISS dose rate for a 2015 day with South Atlantic Anomaly spikes, labelled historical (the dataset ends in 2018). *In the app:* the radiation hand-off example on #/band; #/radiation.
- **NASA GSFC SPDF SSCWeb** (served via hapi-server.org): predicted ISS track and model field strength. Our B < 23,000 nT rule for a South Atlantic Anomaly pass is a design choice. *In the app:* #/radiation.
- **NASA CCMC DONKI** (via api.nasa.gov): 214 solar energetic particle (SEP) entries since 2024-01-01, one per instrument channel or model, so one event often has several, as context: 129 come from instruments and 85 from models only, although the #/data card files the whole snapshot as measured. The RAD replay lists the instrument entries near each trigger, leaves the model ones out, and claims none as the same event (none is logged near 20 May 2024). *In the app:* #/data, #/radiation.
- **NASA, STS-34 mission page:** launch and landing times, orbit and the five astronauts of Atlantis, quoted verbatim and re-checked at every build; launch and landing are converted to UTC from the page's own time zones. With them, #/radiation shows that the replayed October 1989 event began, reached S3 and peaked during that flight, inside Earth's magnetosphere; no dose is attributed to STS-34. *In the app:* #/radiation.
- **NASA Technical Reports Server.** ExMC, *Medical System Concept of Operations for Mars Exploration Mission-11* (NTRS 20200001715): the BAND's gap statement. Maida, *Introduction to the Solid State Based Interior Lighting System for ISS* (NTRS 20160005080): the ISS lighting modes behind the sleep case's phase-shift alternative. *In the app:* #/band and the sleep case.
- **NASA NSSDCA Moon Fact Sheet:** mean perigee, semimajor axis and apogee for the lunar light-time envelope, with the arithmetic shown. *In the app:* #/provenance.
- **NASA Horizon Design System** (@nasa-hds/core v0.10.0, CC0): design tokens, with red returned to its Appendix F meaning.

## Space Agency Partner & Other Data

- **Canadian Space Agency, "Monitoring radiation for astronaut health"** (2026-02-26; official challenge resource). Short verbatim extracts: CANS, which the CSA is funding for "continued development" as "a compact and autonomous instrument designed to measure neutron radiation exposure in space", and neutrons at "about 30% of total radiation on the ISS" (the CSA's rounded ISS figure, not a deep-space value). On #/band, CANS is an example of the dedicated radiation instruments the wristband hands off to. *In the app:* #/band, #/data.
- **Canadian Space Agency, "Why do we conduct science experiments in space?"** (official challenge resource). Its deep-space risks include "increased delays in communication with Earth" and "less ability to rely on Earth for food, materials, and emergency medical assistance": LANDFALL's problem, in the CSA's words. It is a general-audience page with no thresholds, so no rule uses it. *In the app:* #/data.
- **CSA Bio-Monitor (Hexoskin Astroskin)**, flown on the ISS: the BAND's reference hardware. Its facts and channel rates (ECG 256 Hz, PPG 64 Hz, SpO₂ 1 Hz, skin temperature 1 Hz, HRV over 300 s) were read on 2026-11-14 in public sources (a NASA ISS Daily Summary Report, NTRS 20190001996, Hexoskin's product page and API documentation) and are not frozen in this build. Its blood pressure is systolic-only and derived from pulse transit time, so the BAND hands blood pressure to the cuff. *In the app:* #/band.
- **NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center.** The Solar Radiation Storm scale (S1–S5, ≥10 MeV, 5-minute averages) with its biological text verbatim (S3: "Radiation hazard avoidance recommended for astronauts on EVA"), used as OPS rules with chip NOAA-published (scale thresholds, shown as "at or above" a level, not as limits); the GOES proton feed, frozen at build and shown as stale by design ([V2 10020]); and NOAA's list of solar proton events, 1976–2017 (NASA GSFC Solar Data Analysis Center mirror). *In the app:* #/provenance, #/data.
- **NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information.** GOES-7 Energetic Particle Sensor, 5-minute averages, October 1989 (the corrected >10 MeV column): the replayed event, time-shifted only, with no correction for distance from the Sun or for the Martian atmosphere (a labelled scenario assumption). In this file the flux reaches 10 pfu at 13:10 UT on 19 October and 1,000 pfu at 18:55 UT, and peaks at 42,200 pfu at 16:00 UT on 20 October; NOAA's SPE list gives 40,000 pfu. We keep both and adjust neither. *In the app:* scenario, #/radiation, #/data.
- **Peer-reviewed literature.** Crawford & Howell 1998 (the Tier B test) and Crawford et al. 2006 (its behaviour with non-normal data); Jacobson & Truax 1991 (the "reliable change" question; our earlier use of their arithmetic on a within-person baseline is what the audit replaced); Anscombe 1948 (the count transform); Basner, Mollicone & Dinges 2011 (PVT-B; the 355 ms lapse is a post-hoc threshold the authors say may depend on PVT hardware, and NASA-STD-3001 does not adopt it); Barger et al. 2014 (6.09 h of sleep per night in flight by actigraphy, which calibrates the simulation, with the within-person SDs listed in CITATIONS.md); Löwe et al. 2025 (the MSL RAD team's nowcasting rule and its Table 1, quoted from the arXiv preprint); Golightly et al. 1992 (the solar particle events during STS-28 and STS-34); Lang et al. 2004 (bone-loss rates, shown as literature constants); Schäfer & Vagedes 2013 (pulse-rate variability from PPG, behind the BAND's heart rate); Whitson et al. 2009 (potassium citrate, named only in the renal packet's note for the flight surgeon, never to the crew).
- **Standards and parts.** HL7 FHIR R4 (vital-signs profile), LOINC and UCUM for the ground packet; the BIPM SI Brochure for the speed of light; FDA De Novo DEN180044 for the BAND's wrist-and-finger spot ECG. ## Use of Artificial Intelligence

[AUTHORSHIP-TBC] We built LANDFALL with Claude Code (Anthropic) as the coding assistant. We set the scope and the design, made the decisions and reviewed the results. Under our direction, Claude Code:

- wrote most of the code: the Python engine and its tests, the ingest adapters, the TypeScript port, the front end, the cue-card printer and the BAND generator;
- ran the build-time data ingestion;
- ran verification agents that re-read primary sources (standards PDFs, NTRS reports, OSDR files, NOAA, JPL and iSWA data, the Löwe et al. preprint) against our claims, and audited the statistics;
- drafted the documentation, including drafts of this page.

During preparation, AI research agents also searched and summarised background sources.

We treated AI output as claims to check. The checks caught real errors:

- a wrong approval date for NASA-STD-3001 Vol 2 Rev F in our own plan;
- a urine-output target in the plan with no NASA source, replaced by the HRP evidence report's numbers;
- a quote credited to NASA's Standard Measures page that the page does not contain;
- an AI web-page summary that invented an OCHMO "Wearables & Sensors" section (since then, every snapshot the app ships is downloaded and parsed by code);
- the Tier B false-alarm understatements described above, which earlier builds printed;
- a brief that called two RAD triggers "under 1 µGy" (they total 1.4 and 1.2 µGy);
- a draft that cut NASA's IMM limitation sentence short, before "but it may be applied to other design reference missions".

No AI runs inside LANDFALL. There is no language model in the decision path: every rule is data you can read at #/provenance, evaluated by deterministic code that gives the same outputs in Python and in the browser. No image, audio or video generator was used: the body figure and the BAND diagram are SVG drawn by code, the figures are screen captures of the app, and the demo video is captured from the app by a script, with HTML title cards and no audio [VIDEO-TBC]. AI-assisted checking does not replace review by subject-matter experts, which is why every number links to its source.
